Where quality is lost

Biggest

Texture: 2048 px from Tripo becomes 256 px

The raw mesh carries a 2048 px texture with strong dark moss against light bark. We re-unwrap the simplified mesh into many small islands, bake at 1024 px, shrink to 256 px and then grade toward the vanilla palette. Each step averages contrast away. Compare stage 3 with stage 5, and the three textures at the bottom.

Bug

The first bake misses the surface in places

Stage 4 has black patches: the bake's ray distance was set for the old, larger scale, so rays fall short of the detailed mesh. The final rebake covers it up, but it is a sign the bake settings need fixing.

Shape

1.48 million triangles become about 3,000

A 500× reduction turns the knobbly shell and rope binding into smooth blobs. Vanilla brutes are only ~1,200 triangles and still read well, because their texture is crisp. So shape loss matters less than texture loss.

Stage 3

Tripo raw mesh

The model file Tripo returns, before we touch it. The real file is 1.48 million triangles (43 MB). It is shown here reduced to 300,000 so it fits on a web page; that reduction is invisible at this size.

Stage 4

Cut to 3k triangles + first bake

We collapse the mesh to about 3,000 triangles, unwrap it again, and bake the raw mesh's colour onto it at 1024 px, then shrink that to 256 px with nearest-pixel filtering, like the vanilla creatures.

Triangles
3,003
Texture
256 × 256
Look for: the black patches are the bake bug (rays too short). The shell has lost its knobbly outline.
Stage 5

Cleaned, rigged, rebaked

Scaled to vanilla height, fitted to the greyling skeleton, then cleaned up automatically: the club is locked to the hand, the arms are cut free of the shell (no web between them), the legs are slimmed, the face is put on the head bone. Then a second bake onto the finished shape.

Stage 6

Game skin

The texture the game loads: the final bake, colour-graded 90% toward the vanilla greyling palette so it sits with Valheim's art. In game the brute is then scaled ×1.875 onto the brute skeleton and lit by the game.

The textures, laid flat

A texture is the model's skin unfolded onto a square. More of the square spent on visible body means more detail. These are shown with sharp pixels, the way the game draws them.
